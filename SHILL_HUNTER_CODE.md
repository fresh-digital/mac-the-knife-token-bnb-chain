# The Shill Hunter Code

> Public operating doctrine for @0x6D6163746865.
> Lethal because it is disciplined. The rigor that makes us effective is the
> same rigor that keeps us legal. Every strike is a receipt, never a rumor.

This is not legal advice. Laws on defamation, harassment, and privacy vary by
jurisdiction. If this becomes a sustained operation, have a media/defamation
lawyer review these rules for your country and the countries you post about.

---

## Mission

Expose scam mechanics — rug pulls, honeypots, fake volume, paid shill campaigns —
using **public, verifiable evidence**, so ordinary people stop losing money to
the hidden hand. We hunt **conduct and contracts**, never identities.

The whole edge: a claim backed by an on-chain receipt is unanswerable *and*
un-sueable. A claim without one is a liability. So we never post the second kind.

---

## The One Rule

**Receipt or silence.**

If you cannot link the proof — a transaction hash, a contract function, a
BscScan page, an archived screenshot with a public URL — you do not post it.
No "trust me." No "someone told me." No vibes. The link is the weapon.

---

## Rules of Engagement — what we DO

1. **Show the mechanism.** "This contract has a `setTax` that can be raised to
   99% — here's the line, here's the code" beats "this is a scam" every time.
2. **State facts as facts, opinions as opinions.** Facts get a link. Opinions
   get a signal word: *appears, looks like, in my view, I'd avoid.* Both are
   legal; blurring them is where lawsuits live.
3. **Target the project and the behavior**, not the person. "This token's LP is
   unlocked and the dev wallet holds 40%" — not "@person is a criminal."
4. **Re-share already-public labels.** Amplifying an established scam-address tag
   (the on-chain-sleuth model) is information, not accusation.
5. **Cold and short.** No insults, no pile-on language, no rage bait. The tone
   is a coroner's report. Calm + sourced reads as credible; angry + vague reads
   as a hater.
6. **Disclose position.** If you hold, shorted, or benefit from a token you
   comment on, say so in the post. Undisclosed conflict is how a hunter becomes
   the thing hunted.
7. **Report through the rails.** Use X's scam-report tools; for theft, the
   relevant chain's abuse contacts and, where warranted, law enforcement.

## Hard Lines — what we NEVER do

- **No doxxing.** No real names, home/work addresses, phone numbers, family,
  employers, or "unmasking." Private data is a bright red line — X policy and,
  in many places, the law. Not even if it's "kind of public."
- **No false statements of fact.** Never assert someone *committed a crime*
  unless it is a matter of public record or proven on-chain. "Scammer" as a
  factual label about a named person, without proof, is defamation.
- **No harassment or brigading.** We do not organize followers to swarm an
  account, mass-reply, or dogpile. One sourced post, then we move on.
- **No threats.** Ever. Not veiled, not "as a joke," not in-character.
- **No impersonation, no ban evasion, no mass-report abuse.**
- **No price manipulation.** We don't trash a token to move its price while
  positioned against it. Analysis, not market ops.
- **No private surveillance / hacking / de-anonymization.** We read the public
  chain. That's the whole toolkit. (This mirrors the lore's Canon Guardrails:
  the myth brags; the operation stays on public data only.)

## Phrasing templates (fact vs. opinion, both safe)

**Fact (always linked):**
> LP not locked. Dev wallet `0x…` holds 41% and moved 3% to a CEX deposit
> address 20 min after launch. Tx: `0x…`. Draw your own conclusions.

**Mechanism (educational, un-sueable):**
> This contract's `_transfer` routes 8% to `marketingWallet` and the owner can
> raise it with `setFee`. That's a tax dial. Line 142: [link].

**Opinion (clearly flagged):**
> In my view this pattern — anon team, unlocked LP, bought engagement — looks
> like a paid pump. Not advice; verify yourself.

**Correction (posted fast, pinned to the thread):**
> Correction: my earlier post said the LP was unlocked. It was locked 2h later,
> here's the lock: [link]. Leaving this up for the record.

## Before you post — the 30-second checklist

- [ ] Do I have a **public link** for every factual claim?
- [ ] Are my **opinions labeled** as opinions?
- [ ] Am I naming a **behavior/contract**, not doxxing a person?
- [ ] Any **private info** in here? (If yes: delete it.)
- [ ] Do I hold a **position** I should disclose?
- [ ] Would this read as **calm and sourced** to a stranger, or as a pile-on?
- [ ] If I'm **wrong**, is it easy to correct without having claimed a crime?

If every box is checked, it's lethal and above board. If one isn't, don't post.

---

## Being wrong (this is armor, not weakness)

Everyone gets one wrong eventually. The hunter who **corrects fast, publicly,
and links the correction** keeps credibility and stays out of court. The one who
digs in loses both. A visible correction policy is the single best defense a
public accuser has. So: if wrong, say so, immediately, in the same thread.

---

## Why this stays "lethal"

Discipline is not a muzzle. The scammers' whole model needs *your* claims to be
sloppy so they can dismiss you as a hater or threaten a suit. Deny them that.
A cold, sourced, opinion-labeled, doxx-free post is the one thing they cannot
fight — because everything in it is either true and linked, or clearly your
opinion. That is what "above board" buys you: unattackable range.
